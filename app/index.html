Guided setup for your avatar, companion, and first World Cartridge.

Offline-first world shell
Step 1 of 128%

Step 1

Prepare your World Shard

Start from scratch, load a world theme pack, load a companion pack, or load both. Startup and /loader should land in the same final game state.

Step 2

Name and class your world

Set the world name first, then tell the shell what trope family and visual metaphor lane this world lives in.

Step 3

Describe the world and launch it

Give the world one short hard reminder. Keep it under a social post so it can be flashed back to the model cleanly every turn.

Step 4

Name your companion cleanly

Set the core companion identity first. Separate first and last name so the shell never has to guess them.

Scroll for the rest of the companion identity fields.

Step 5

Anchor their body and presence

These are hard visual and physical anchors. They help the shell keep the companion stable from scene to scene.

Scroll for the rest of the body and wardrobe anchors.

Step 6

Set how the companion performs

Now decide how they talk, how much they lead, and what stable first-glance profile the model should enter with.

Scroll for the rest of the performance and reference fields.

Step 7

Build the personality profile

Pick up to three trope cues. The shell will scramble them into three quick personality profiles, then you choose the one you like best.

Scroll this list for more personality lanes.

Step 8

Weight the personality gears

Pick up to three personality lanes, then set how hard each one runs. These weights act like behavior levers, so keep them direct and deliberate.

Scroll for the slider mix, then shape the balance you actually want in play.

Step 9

Set the companion dynamic

This writes the inner script for your companion. This is the story they tell themselves that shapes how they play with you. Do not be vague. Write directives.

Step 10

Set their quirks and tendencies

This is Behavior Emphasis: your companion's "isms." Quirks, tendencies, gestures, expressions, and the ways they describe certain things. It shapes how they move and sound in every scene.

Step 11

Set up your avatar

Now that companion data is locked in, fill in the player profile the shell uses for cards and prompt context.

Step 12

Describe your avatar’s presence

Keep these readable and short. These give the shell a stable player-side visual card and in-scene reminder packet.

Step 13

Choose your shell theme and mood image

Set the room/ASCII shell look first, then pick a starter background or upload your own. These are shell presentation layers only.

Scroll for theme controls and more backgrounds.
